The research behind the concept

From clinical immersion to product design

The concept originated from observing staffing coordination at two acute-care hospitals during McMaster University's Master of Biomedical Innovation program.

01 · Uneven staffing coverage

Observed nurse-to-patient ratios varied substantially between units, including examples around 1:3 and 1:7–1:8. These observations suggested a need for clearer cross-unit capacity visibility, while recognizing that safe staffing depends on acuity and specialization.

02 · Repetitive reporting

Twice-daily staffing huddles lasted approximately 30–40 minutes. NurseAlign proposes structured digital reporting for routine census, admissions, and discharge updates, preserving clinical discussion for decisions requiring judgment.

03 · Competency before availability

An available nurse is not automatically an appropriate nurse for every unit. The proposed matching workflow places competency requirements alongside availability and overtime considerations.

04 · Prototype, not deployment

This website demonstrates product workflows with fictional data. It is not connected to hospital systems, does not run a validated clinical matching model, and has not been evaluated as a production clinical application.
